# Project intake

Ask only what supplied materials do not answer. Combine unresolved decisions into one short message, offering sensible options where useful. A still may need subject, style, dimensions, source assets, and destination; a film needs timing and audio decisions too. Start independent inspection while awaiting an answer.

## Interview

1. **Outcome and audience:** What should the viewer understand or feel? Is this one image, a sequence, a film, or multiple edits? Where will people see it?
2. **Subject and sources:** What existing CAD, meshes, photos, storyboards, logos, or references are authoritative? Which may be altered? Who owns or licenses each external asset?
3. **Truth requirements:** Is this promotional illustration, explanatory visualization, engineering-accurate depiction, or simulation evidence? Which dimensions, mechanisms, labels, materials, and behavior must be faithful? Which details are intentionally illustrative?
4. **Creative direction:** What visual references, mood, lighting, camera movement, color, level of realism, and elements to avoid matter most? What is already approved?
5. **Outputs:** Aspect ratios, resolution, frame rate, duration or timing constraints, still/video format, color space, subtitles, and platform. If unknown, propose a small set appropriate to the destination after checking its current rules.
6. **Sound:** Music, effects, narration, exact script, language, voice choice, licensing, and whether silent deliverables are needed. Do not generate a new voice or rewrite approved wording without resolving the choice.
7. **Production limits:** Deadline, budget or paid-compute ceiling, available machine or cloud resources, storage and delivery location, review owner, and any required approval milestone.

Do not demand every answer before making progress. Mark assumptions in the brief and ask when a missing answer could change the model, story, cost, or final export.

For each shot, record its purpose, action, start/end or duration, camera and framing, source revision, effects, sound or text, and acceptance check. Keep one current brief; archive superseded choices without treating them as active requirements.
